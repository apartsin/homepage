These are my earlier publications (2024 and before), grouped by career stage from my B.Sc. through my industry research, spanning computer vision, signal processing, and applied machine learning. For 2025 onward, see Recent Publications; the complete and continuously updated list is on Google Scholar.

  1. B.Sc. St. Petersburg State University / Technion
  2. 1998 Journal

    A circular graph: counterexample to the Duchet kernel conjecture

    A. Apartsin, E. Ferapontova, V. Gurvich

    Discrete Mathematics, 178(1-3)

    Constructs a circular graph that serves as a counterexample to Duchet's kernel conjecture in directed graph theory. The result settles an open combinatorics question by exhibiting a concrete structure that the conjecture had failed to rule out.

  3. M.Sc. Weizmann Institute, Computer Vision
  4. 2005 Conference

    Multiscale Segmentation by Combining Motion and Intensity Cues

    M. Galun, A. Apartsin, R. Basri

    IEEE Conf. Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition (CVPR)

    Segments images and video by fusing motion and intensity cues within a single multiscale framework. Combining the two cues separates objects that either signal alone would leave ambiguous, sharpening the segmentation of moving structures.

  5. Ph.D. Tel Aviv University, Machine Learning & Signal Processing (2008-2012)
  6. 2014 Journal

    Energy-Efficient Time-of-Flight Estimation in the Presence of Outliers: A Machine Learning Approach

    A. Apartsin, L.N. Cooper, N. Intrator

    IEEE J. Selected Topics in Applied Earth Observations, 7(4)

    Casts robust, low-power time-of-flight estimation as a machine learning problem. Learning from data reaches accurate ranging with weak pulses, cutting energy use while tolerating outliers.

  7. 2013 Journal

    Time-of-flight estimation in the presence of outliers. Part II: Multiple echo processing

    A. Apartsin, L.N. Cooper, N. Intrator

    IEEE Trans. Geoscience and Remote Sensing, 52(7)

    Extends outlier-robust time-of-flight estimation to settings with multiple overlapping echoes. Jointly processing several echoes resolves closely spaced returns that single-echo methods cannot separate.

  8. 2013 Journal

    Time-of-flight estimation in the presence of outliers. Part I: Single echo processing

    A. Apartsin, L.N. Cooper, N. Intrator

    IEEE Trans. Geoscience and Remote Sensing, 52(6)

    Estimates time of flight from a single echo while explicitly rejecting outlier measurements. Handling outliers directly keeps range estimates stable when stray reflections or noise spikes corrupt the signal.

  9. 2012 Journal

    Semi-coherent time of arrival estimation using regression

    A. Apartsin, L.N. Cooper, N. Intrator

    J. Acoustical Society of America, 132(2)

    Estimates time of arrival with a semi-coherent regression method that blends coherent and incoherent processing. The regression formulation improves timing accuracy while staying robust to the phase uncertainty of noisy returns.

  10. 2011 Conference

    Biosonar-inspired source localization in low SNR

    A. Apartsin, L.N. Cooper, N. Intrator

    Int. Conf. Bio-inspired Systems and Signal Processing

    Draws on biological sonar strategies to localize a source under very low signal-to-noise ratios. The bio-inspired design pinpoints targets in regimes where conventional matched filtering breaks down under heavy noise.

  11. 2010 Conference

    SNR-dependent filtering for Time Of Arrival estimation in high noise

    A. Apartsin, L.N. Cooper, N. Intrator

    IEEE Workshop on Machine Learning for Signal Processing

    Adapts the filtering stage to the local signal-to-noise ratio when estimating a pulse's time of arrival. Tuning the filter to noise conditions keeps arrival-time estimates accurate even far below the classical detection threshold.

  12. 2008 Journal

    A data fusion and multiple ping method for improving the resolution of low-power acoustic and seismic sensing

    A. Apartsin, N. Intrator

    J. Acoustical Society of America, 124(4)

    Combines multiple low-power pings through data fusion to sharpen range resolution in acoustic and seismic sensing. Fusing repeated weak pulses recovers detail that would otherwise demand a single high-power ping, keeping the sensing covert and energy-efficient.

  13. Postdoctoral Weizmann Institute, Computer Vision
  14. 2013 Conference

    Accurate Blur Models vs. Image Priors in Single Image Super-Resolution

    N. Efrat, D. Glasner, A. Apartsin, B. Nadler, A. Levin

    IEEE Int. Conf. Computer Vision (ICCV)

    Examines whether an accurate blur model or a strong image prior matters more for single-image super-resolution. The study shows that getting the blur model right is often the decisive factor, reframing how super-resolution pipelines should be built.

  15. Industry Applied AI research, 2018-2022
  16. 2022 Conference

    Early Detection of Engine Anomalies: A Case Study for AI-based Integrated Vehicle Health Management

    A. Apartsin, H. Stein, G. Reiter, K. Williams, N. Moscovich

    SAE WCX World Congress, SAE Technical Paper 2022-01-0225

    Presents an AI-based integrated vehicle health management system that detects engine anomalies early from telematics signals. The case study shows how learned models flag developing faults before they escalate, supporting predictive maintenance across a fleet.

  17. 2021 Conference

    AI-based Signal Integrity Monitoring for Integrated Vehicle Health Management

    A. Apartsin, H. Stein, G. Reiter, K. Williams

    VDI ELIV 2021 (VDI-Berichte 2384), pp. 143-150

    Introduces AI-based monitoring of signal integrity as part of integrated vehicle health management. Continuously checking signal quality lets the system trust its own inputs and catch sensor and data faults before they mislead downstream models.

  18. 2018 Conference

    Robust Motion Compensation for Forensic Analysis of Egocentric Video using Joint Stabilization and Tracking

    O. Cohen, A. Apartsin, J. Alon, E. Katz

    IEEE Int. Conf. Science of Electrical Engineering (ICSEE)

    Jointly stabilizes and tracks egocentric video to compensate for large camera motion in forensic analysis. Coupling stabilization with tracking keeps targets followable in shaky first-person footage where either step alone would drift.
