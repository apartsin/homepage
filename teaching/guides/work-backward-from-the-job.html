Some of this will sound obvious, and yet many graduates skip it. Employers do not hire “Computer Science graduates” in the abstract; they hire for specific roles. Work backward from the role you want, close the gaps on purpose, and arrive at graduation already recognizable to employers.

How to prepare for the job market

Five steps that turn “I have a CS degree” into “I am ready for this specific role.”

1

Choose your target roles

Employers rarely post jobs for “Computer Science graduates.” They recruit for specific roles: Junior Front-End Developer, Full-Stack Engineer, Data Engineer, AI/LLM Engineer. Pick the roles you actually want and focus your preparation on them.

2

Understand what employers actually expect

Read a few dozen real job listings for your target roles. Identify the recurring technical skills, tools, frameworks, and types of practical experience employers ask for. You can use ChatGPT to help analyze listings and pull out the recurring requirements.

3

Build a structured learning plan

Compare your current skills with those requirements and name the gaps. Make a realistic plan to close them. Prefer quality online courses (for example on Coursera) with real assignments, exams, and recognized certifications. Aim for actual mastery, not a stack of “completed” courses.

4

Build a convincing project portfolio

Design projects that actually demonstrate the required skills. Choose projects with genuine technical depth, meaningful challenges, and substantial use of the relevant technologies. A few strong projects beat many superficial ones. Be ready to explain your architectural decisions, implementation challenges, and technical contributions.

5

Learn to present and defend your work

Being able to build something is not enough; you also have to explain what you built, why you made each decision, what alternatives you considered, and what you learned. Use ChatGPT to review your projects, challenge your technical decisions, simulate interviews, and critique your answers before a real interviewer does.

The key principle: do not wait until graduation to discover what the market expects. Work backward from your target role: identify the requirements, master the skills, demonstrate them through projects, and practice explaining your work convincingly.
