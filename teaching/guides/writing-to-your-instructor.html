How to Write to Your Instructor

English עברית

Writing a good message to your instructor is a skill worth practicing. A clear, self-contained, and respectful question earns a faster and better answer, and the effort of framing it often teaches you as much as the reply.

Five habits for a good message

Before you send, and what to expect back.

1

Try to answer it yourself first

Treat each question as a chance to learn independently, especially how to find and validate information on your own, a skill you will certainly need later. You can always ask the instructor, but if you ask before trying, you miss the chance to build that skill, which is the whole point of the course and the degree.

2

Include everything needed to understand it

Give all the information your question needs. Do not assume the instructor remembers an earlier conversation, and do not leave out the background that is critical to understanding what you are asking.

3

Be brief, and word it with care

You are asking for the instructor's time, so show that you invested your own in making the question clear and short. Aim for a single paragraph, choose your wording carefully, and add links for any background that is needed. Framing the question correctly is already most of the way to the answer.

4

Stay polite and professional

Keep the language polite and professional, and reread the message for wording that could accidentally come across as disrespectful or inappropriate.

5

Expect links or an AI transcript in reply

Be ready to receive links to resources or a transcript of an AI conversation. It is often the easiest way for the instructor to give you a complete, well-structured answer.
