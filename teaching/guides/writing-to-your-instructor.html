How to Write to Your Instructor

English עברית

Writing a good message to your instructor is a skill worth practicing. A clear, self-contained, and respectful question earns a faster and better answer, and the effort of framing it often teaches you as much as the reply.

Five habits for a good message

Before you send, and what to expect back. Each habit has the trap to avoid.

1

Try to answer it yourself first

Treat each question as a chance to practice finding and checking information on your own, a skill you will need long after the course. You can always ask afterward.

The trap: asking before you have tried, skipping the exact skill the course exists to build.

2

Include everything needed to understand it

Give all the context the question needs to stand on its own.

The trap: “it still doesn’t work, what should I do?” with no code, error, or reminder of which project you mean.

3

Be brief, and word it with care

Aim for a single clear paragraph, with links for any background. Framing the question well is already most of the answer.

The trap: a rambling wall of text that makes the reader do the work of figuring out what you actually need.

4

Stay polite and professional

Keep it courteous, and reread once for wording that could land wrong.

The trap: a terse, demanding note fired off in frustration, “why wasn’t this explained?”

5

Expect links or an AI transcript in reply

You may get links or a transcript of an AI conversation. It is often the quickest way to give you a complete, well-structured answer.

The trap: reading it as a brush-off instead of a resource, and not actually working through what you were sent.
