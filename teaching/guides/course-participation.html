The Recordings Are Not the Course

Home / Teaching / Guides for Students / The Recordings Are Not the Course
English עברית

I usually record the sessions so that a student who misses a class can watch it later. But treat the recording as a safety net, not the reason to enroll: the real value of the course is in the room, not in the playback.

Where the value actually is

Why the lectures are the least important part, and when the course is not a fit.

1

Watching the lectures is the least important part

Most of the material can also be found on YouTube, Coursera, elsewhere online, or through AI tools. The lectures are useful, but they are not where the course’s unique value lives.

2

The value comes from interaction

Asking questions, presenting your project’s progress, discussing ideas, and receiving feedback. That exchange happens live, with me and with your peers, and a recording cannot give it back to you.

3

You present in class three times

Every project group is required to present in class three times during the semester: the project proposal, the midterm progress, and the final presentation.

4

Reconsider if you mainly plan to watch the recordings

If your plan is mostly to watch the Zoom recordings, this may not be the right course for you. You would miss much of its value while still investing the full effort the project demands.
